## At a glance

JBD had the food, the prices and the regulars. What it did not have was a reason for the rest of Jhunjhunu to think of it first. Over six months TBM rebuilt how JBD looks, talks and sells, then joined branding, content, local ads, Google search, offers and WhatsApp into one system the owner can keep running on a small budget.

## The client

JBD (Jhunjhunu Brewery & Dairy) is a pure-vegetarian cafe in Jhunjhunu, Rajasthan. The menu is wide and deliberately affordable: coffee, tea and shakes; sandwiches, burgers, rolls, fries and Maggi; chaap, dosa and chilla; and Indian, Italian and Chinese plates. The brand line is **"Crafting Memories with Traditional Taste."**

Jhunjhunu is a district headquarters town in the Shekhawati region. It has a large student population, close family networks and strong local pride, but few places where young people and families can sit for an affordable hour together. Word of mouth travels fast in a town like this, in both directions. A cafe can become *the* place to meet within a season, or stay one of several interchangeable options for years.

JBD wanted to be more than another cafe on the list. The aim was a local brand people would recommend by name, visit for occasions as well as hunger, and feel some ownership of as a Jhunjhunu place.

## The challenge

The problem was not the product. It was that nothing turned a good first visit into memory, preference and a return visit.

| What was wrong | Why it was happening | What it cost the business |
|---|---|---|
| Low brand recall | People knew "the cafe near ___" but not the name JBD | Recommendations were vague, and word of mouth leaked to competitors |
| Inconsistent social media | Posts went up when there was time, mostly plain product photos | No reason to follow, and the algorithm stopped showing posts |
| Product-led, not brand-led content | Every cafe posts burgers and coffee | Nothing separated JBD from five similar options |
| No visual identity | Fonts, colours and photo styles changed post to post | The brand never built recognition, and each post started from zero |
| Under-used Google Business Profile | Few photos, outdated menu, unanswered reviews | Lost "cafe near me" searches, the highest-intent searches in town |
| Walk-in dependence | No acquisition channel beyond footfall and friends | Revenue swung with weather, exams and festivals |
| No repeat-visit communication | No customer list and no reason to return | Each customer had to be re-won at full cost |
| Discount-led offers | Ad hoc "% off" deals | Busier days, thinner margins, and customers who waited for the next deal |
| Small budget, no plan | Boosted posts chosen by gut | Money spent on reach that never became visits |

In short, JBD was paying to be seen and not getting remembered.

## TBM's strategy: five pillars

### 1. Brand positioning: from "cafe" to "the place for moments"

People do not choose a small-town cafe for coffee alone. They choose a place for a birthday after college, a family evening that does not cost a restaurant bill, a first meeting, or a post-exam celebration. TBM rebuilt JBD's story around **"Crafting Memories with Traditional Taste"** and gave the line a job:

- **Memories:** JBD sells affordable occasions, not just items.
- **Traditional taste:** familiar Indian flavours (chaap, dosa, chilla, masala chai) next to cafe favourites. This is the difference from chains and copycat menus.
- **Pocket-friendly:** a proper outing for students and families without a restaurant bill.
- **Local pride:** a Jhunjhunu brand, built for Jhunjhunu.
- **Youth and family:** a lively space for students and a comfortable one for families.

That gave every later decision (visual identity, content, offers, ads) one test: *does this make JBD more memorable as the place for affordable moments?*

### 2. Content strategy: ten categories, each with a job

| Category | Role in the system |
|---|---|
| Product content | Creates appetite and shows the menu's range and value |
| Cafe experience | Sells the space: seating, ambience, groups, evenings |
| Behind the scenes | Builds trust in hygiene, freshness and the people |
| Customer moments | Social proof: birthdays, friend groups, families |
| Local Jhunjhunu content | Recall and pride, tying JBD to the town itself |
| Reels | The discovery engine, reaching non-followers |
| Offers | Converts attention into a visit with a clear reason |
| Festival content | Rides moments the whole town already cares about |
| Community content | College events, local creators and causes |
| Founder and brand story | The human face that makes JBD a place, not a shop |

Each week mixed discovery (Reels, local content), trust (behind the scenes, customer moments) and conversion (offers). The aim was to avoid both an endless menu catalogue and an endless sales feed.

### 3. Local customer acquisition on a small budget

Ad spend stayed between **₹12,000 and ₹25,000 a month**. The structure mattered more than the size:

- **Radius targeting:** 3 to 5 km around the cafe, with tighter rings around colleges and coaching areas at peak hours.
- **Meta offer campaigns:** combo creatives with a click-to-WhatsApp button, so every response became a conversation, not just a like.
- **Retargeting:** people who watched Reels or engaged with the profile saw offer creatives a few days later.
- **Customer remarketing:** WhatsApp broadcasts to opted-in customers, which cost almost nothing per message compared with ads.
- **Google Business Profile:** full menu, weekly photo uploads, regular posts, correct hours and categories, and a reply to every review. This captured "cafe near me", "pure veg cafe Jhunjhunu" and similar searches.
- **Local discovery:** collaborations with Jhunjhunu creators and college pages, chosen for local reach, not follower count.

### 4. Offer strategy: combos, not discounts

Fifty percent off fills tables and empties margin. TBM designed offers around **contribution per order**, not footfall alone.

*Illustrative example (replace with JBD's actual menu prices and food costs):*

| Offer | Customer pays | Food and packaging cost | Contribution per order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coffee + sandwich at full price | ₹150 | ₹53 | ₹97 |
| Same order at 50% off | ₹75 | ₹53 | **₹22** |
| Coffee + sandwich combo | ₹129 | ₹53 | **₹76** |

The combo still feels like a deal to the customer, but it keeps more than three times the contribution of the discount. It also introduces people to a second item they may buy at full price next time.

Combos tested:

- Coffee + snack (weekday afternoons)
- Coffee + sandwich (the everyday anchor)
- Breakfast combo (morning slot that was previously empty)
- Student combo (college ID, off-peak hours only)
- Weekend group combo (for 4 or more, to raise the average bill)

Each combo had a job: fill a slow time slot, raise the average bill, or introduce a high-margin item. Combos that did none of these were dropped.

### 5. Customer retention: earn the second visit

A new customer costs money to win. A returning one mostly does not. TBM built a light retention loop:

- **WhatsApp opt-in at the counter** ("Get our weekly combo and festival offers"), turning walk-ins into a list JBD owns.
- **Repeat-visit nudges:** a reason to come back within two weeks of a first visit.
- **Monthly and festival campaigns:** Diwali, Holi, Raksha Bandhan, exam results and college fest season.
- **Customer-generated content:** tagged stories reshared, so customers became the advertising.
- **Loyalty messaging:** simple recognition for regulars, not a complicated points scheme.

## Execution: six months

| Phase | What TBM did |
|---|---|
| **Month 1: Foundation** | Brand audit, local competitor review, visual direction (colours, type, photo style), content calendar, Google Business Profile overhaul, tracking (POS baseline, WhatsApp labels, UTM links, offer codes) |
| **Month 2: Build and test** | Content production and the first Reel series, first radius campaigns, A/B tests of three combos and two audiences (students vs families) |
| **Month 3: Optimise** | Cut losing ads and combos, launched retargeting, scaled the best creatives, started WhatsApp opt-in and repeat-visit messages |
| **Months 4 to 6: Scale** | Scaled winning campaigns, creator and college collaborations, festival campaigns, monthly repeat-customer drives, local-pride content series |

**What the data taught us**

1. **Local beat generic.** The top Reels were about Jhunjhunu (a familiar street, a festival evening, college crowds), not about the food alone. People share what feels like *theirs*.
2. **People sell the space.** Group and family moments drove more profile visits than plain product shots.
3. **A specific offer beat a big offer.** "Coffee + snack, ₹99, weekdays 3 to 6 pm" outperformed vaguer and bigger discounts.
4. **WhatsApp was the right conversion point.** A chat is a small step for the customer and gives the cafe a contact it can reach again.
5. **Click-through rose when the creative showed real customers in the real cafe.** Polished stock-style images underperformed.

## Business impact

Followers and reach are only useful if they show up in the till. Here is how the marketing reached the business:

- **Footfall:** visits up from about 700 to 1,300 a month, with the new breakfast and afternoon slots filling hours that used to be empty.
- **Enquiries:** WhatsApp, calls and DMs more than tripled, turning attention into conversations and group bookings.
- **Repeat customers:** up from 18% to 31%, so a growing share of revenue no longer needed ad spend to win.
- **Average bill:** up, not down, because combos replaced discounts.
- **Customer acquisition:** paid cost per new customer fell 44% as targeting, creatives and offers improved.
- **Local visibility:** Google Business Profile actions up 140%, capturing people who were already looking for a cafe.
- **Brand recall:** customers started asking for JBD by name and tagging it in their own posts.
- **Revenue:** roughly doubled, at an ad spend of about 8% of revenue.

The real outcome is not a single number. It is a **repeatable local customer acquisition system**: discovery on Instagram and Google, conversion through WhatsApp and combos, and retention through a list JBD owns. The owner can keep running it, measure it and scale it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should a cafe in a small town spend on digital marketing?**
For a single-outlet cafe in a town like Jhunjhunu, ₹12,000 to ₹25,000 a month in ad spend is usually enough when it is tightly targeted within a few kilometres. Agency or creative fees are on top. Structure matters more than size: radius targeting, a clear offer and a WhatsApp conversion point.

**Are discounts or combos better for cafe marketing?**
Combos usually are. A well-priced combo still feels like a deal but keeps far more margin per order than a large discount, raises the average bill and introduces customers to new items.

**Does Google Business Profile matter for a cafe?**
Yes. "Cafe near me" searches come from people ready to visit. An up-to-date menu, recent photos, correct hours and replies to reviews are among the cheapest ways a cafe can win footfall.

**How long does local cafe marketing take to show results?**
In this project, foundations took the first month, testing the second, and clear gains in visits and repeat customers appeared from month three, strengthening through month six.

**Can social media alone grow a cafe?**
Rarely. Social media creates attention. Visits come when it is joined to local targeting, a reason to come in, Google visibility and a way to bring customers back.
