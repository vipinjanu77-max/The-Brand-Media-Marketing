## The short answer

<div class="answer"><span class="answer-label">Short answer</span>A good CPL is one below your <strong>break-even CPL</strong>: deal value x gross margin x qualification rate x close rate.</div>

## Why averages mislead

"Average CPL in real estate" or "average CPL for education" hides huge differences in city, price point, offer, channel, form type and (above all) what counts as a lead. A ₹200 lead that never answers the phone is more expensive than a ₹2,000 lead that books a site visit.

## The break-even CPL formula

**Break-even CPL = Deal value x Gross margin % x Qualification rate % x Close rate %**

### Worked example (illustrative numbers)

- Deal value: ₹1,00,000
- Gross margin: 30%, so gross profit per customer is ₹30,000
- 40% of leads are qualified
- 15% of qualified leads buy

Break-even CPL = ₹1,00,000 x 0.30 x 0.40 x 0.15 = **₹1,800**

At a CPL of ₹1,800, marketing just pays for itself on the first sale. A target of perhaps 50-70% of break-even leaves room for profit and for sales and overhead costs: how much headroom you need depends on your cost structure and repeat revenue.

## How to lower cost per customer (not just CPL)

1. **Improve qualification:** add qualifying questions and route leads fast.
2. **Send quality signals to ad platforms:** import qualified-lead and sale events from your CRM.
3. **Improve landing pages:** message match and speed raise conversion rate.
4. **Speed up follow-up:** instant WhatsApp or call-back.
5. **Tighten targeting:** negative keywords, exclusions, better audiences.
