## What is digital marketing?

<div class="answer"><span class="answer-label">Definition</span>Digital marketing is the use of online channels to attract, convert and retain customers, and to measure which activities actually produce revenue.</div>

The channels are the visible part: Google, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, email, websites. The more important part is the system underneath: how a business decides whom to reach, what to say, where to spend, and how it knows whether it worked.

A useful way to think about it:

**Business problem, then strategy, then the marketing system, execution, measurement and growth.**

Most disappointing marketing skips straight to execution: "we need Instagram posts", "let's run Google Ads". The channels then run in isolation, and nobody can say which rupees produced customers.

## How does digital marketing work?

<div class="answer"><span class="answer-label">Short answer</span>Digital marketing works by reaching people where they spend time or search online, giving them a reason to act, capturing that action (a visit, lead or purchase), and using the data to improve the next round.</div>

In practice, it is a loop:

1. **Reach:** get in front of the right people (search results, feeds, videos, inboxes).
2. **Engage:** earn attention with a relevant message or useful content.
3. **Convert:** turn interest into a measurable action on a website, form, call or chat.
4. **Qualify and sell:** sales or checkout turn that action into revenue.
5. **Retain:** keep customers buying, referring and reviewing.
6. **Measure and optimise:** feed outcomes back so budgets and algorithms favour what works.

Modern ad platforms automate much of steps 1-3. That makes step 6 (accurate conversion data) the difference between an algorithm that finds customers and one that finds cheap clicks.

## The main digital marketing channels

| Channel | What it does best | Typical role in the funnel | Time to impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEO** | Earns visibility for searches without paying per click | Research to purchase | Months |
| **AEO / AI search** | Makes you understandable and citable in AI answers | Research and comparison | Months |
| **Google Ads** | Captures people actively searching | Purchase intent | Days to weeks |
| **Meta Ads** (Facebook, Instagram) | Creates demand through visual, interest-based reach | Awareness to purchase | Days to weeks |
| **LinkedIn Ads** | Reaches professionals by role and company | B2B awareness to lead | Weeks |
| **YouTube and video** | Demonstrates, explains and builds trust | Awareness and consideration | Weeks |
| **Social media (organic)** | Builds community, credibility and reach | Awareness and loyalty | Months |
| **Content marketing** | Educates buyers and supports SEO/AEO | All stages | Months |
| **Email and WhatsApp** | Follows up, nurtures and retains | Conversion and retention | Immediate |
| **Website and landing pages** | Converts every channel's traffic | Conversion | Immediate once live |
| **Analytics and CRM** | Connects activity to revenue | Measurement | Foundational |

No business needs every channel. The right mix depends on how your customers buy.

## Digital marketing strategy

<div class="answer"><span class="answer-label">Short answer</span>A digital marketing strategy decides who you want to win, why they should choose you, which channels will reach them efficiently, how much you can afford to pay for a customer, and how you will measure success.</div>

A strategy that holds up usually answers seven questions:

1. **Objective:** What business outcome? (Revenue, qualified pipeline, bookings, market entry.)
2. **Audience:** Who buys, who influences, and what triggers the purchase?
3. **Positioning:** Why you rather than the alternatives, in the customer's words?
4. **Economics:** What can you afford to pay for a lead or customer? (See the worked example below.)
5. **Channels:** Which channels match how your audience buys?
6. **Conversion path:** What happens after the click: page, form, WhatsApp, sales call?
7. **Measurement:** Which numbers decide whether to scale, fix or stop?

## The digital marketing funnel

| Stage | Buyer is asking | Useful content and campaigns | Metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top of funnel (TOFU)** | "Do I have a problem? What are my options?" | Education, research, social and video | Reach, engaged visits |
| **Middle of funnel (MOFU)** | "Which approach or supplier fits?" | Guides, comparisons, tools, case studies, webinars | Leads, MQLs |
| **Bottom of funnel (BOFU)** | "Why you, why now, what will it cost?" | Pricing, proposals, demos, testimonials, search ads | SQLs, customers, CAC |
| **Retention** | "Was this a good decision?" | Onboarding, email/WhatsApp, community | Repeat rate, LTV, reviews |

The funnel is a simplification (real journeys loop and skip) but it prevents a common mistake: judging awareness activity on immediate sales, or bottom-funnel campaigns on reach.

## Budgets and unit economics: a worked example

Budgets should come from economics, not round numbers. Here is an illustrative calculation with hypothetical inputs (not a benchmark):

- Average deal value: ₹50,000; gross margin: 40%, so gross profit per customer is ₹20,000
- Lead-to-customer rate: 5% (1 in 20 leads buys)
- **Maximum affordable cost per lead to break even on the first sale:** ₹20,000 x 5% = **₹1,000**
- To win 20 customers a month you need 400 leads. At ₹800 CPL, that is **₹3,20,000** of media

If your realistic CPL is above ₹1,000, you need to improve conversion, raise prices, increase repeat revenue, or accept a payback period. Our [ROI calculator](/tools/marketing-roi-calculator/) runs this with your own numbers.

## KPIs that matter

- **Business outcomes:** revenue, customers, pipeline value, **CAC** (customer acquisition cost), **LTV** (lifetime value), payback period.
- **Efficiency:** **ROAS** vs break-even ROAS, cost per qualified lead, marketing cost as a share of revenue.
- **Funnel diagnostics:** CTR, CPC, landing-page conversion rate, lead-to-customer rate.

Platform metrics like CTR are diagnostics. They explain *why* results changed, but they are not the goal.

## Digital marketing by business type

**B2B.** Long cycles and buying committees. Prioritise search intent, LinkedIn, authoritative content, CRM integration and pipeline reporting. Measure cost per sales-qualified opportunity, not cost per lead.

**B2C.** Larger audiences and shorter cycles. Creative, offers, reviews and fast conversion paths matter most.

**D2C.** Direct sales depend on creative testing, site conversion, contribution margin after returns, and repeat purchase. ROAS must be judged against break-even ROAS.

**Local businesses.** Google Business Profile, reviews, local search pages and simple call/WhatsApp tracking often outperform complex strategies.

**Enterprise.** Governance, measurement consistency across brands and markets, brand safety and integration with sales and finance systems become central.

## Digital marketing in India vs globally

India has a very large and diverse online population (DataReportal estimates 1.03 billion internet users at the end of 2025, or 70% of the population) and digital is now the majority of advertising spend (an estimated 59% in 2025, according to the dentsu-e4m Digital Advertising Report 2026). See our [India statistics database](/research/digital-marketing-statistics-india/) for sources.

Practical differences marketers in India plan for:

- **Language and region.** Many audiences respond better to regional languages and local context.
- **Mobile-first behaviour.** Pages must load quickly on mid-range phones and variable networks.
- **WhatsApp as a conversion channel.** Many customers prefer to chat rather than fill a long form or call.
- **Payment and trust.** Cash on delivery, returns and reviews shape e-commerce economics.
- **Metro vs tier-2/3 differences.** Channel mix, price sensitivity and content formats can differ sharply.

International campaigns add currency, regulation, privacy law, cultural adaptation and time-zone considerations.

## Benefits and limitations

**Benefits**

- Measurable: you can connect spend to outcomes far more precisely than most offline media.
- Targetable: reach by intent, interest, location, role or behaviour.
- Flexible: start small, test, and scale what works.
- Compounding: SEO, content and reputation build long-term assets.

**Limitations**

- Measurement is imperfect: privacy changes, cross-device journeys and platform self-attribution all distort data.
- Competitive auctions raise costs as more businesses advertise online.
- Automation can optimise for the wrong outcome if fed poor data.
- It cannot fix a weak product, uncompetitive pricing or poor sales follow-up.

## Future trends

- **AI search and answer engines** summarise information rather than listing links, making clarity, evidence and entity consistency more important. See [AEO](/services/aeo/).
- **Automation-first ad platforms** make conversion data and creative the main levers.
- **First-party data and consent** matter more as third-party tracking declines.
- **Video everywhere:** short-form and long-form video are central to discovery.
- **Messaging commerce:** conversations increasingly replace forms, especially in India.

## Where to start

1. Write down your unit economics and what a customer is worth.
2. Fix tracking so you can see leads and sales by source.
3. Start with the channel closest to existing demand (usually search).
4. Build one strong conversion path before adding channels.
5. Review results monthly against business outcomes.
