## The short answer

<div class="answer"><span class="answer-label">Short answer</span>Budget = customers needed ÷ close rate ÷ landing-page conversion rate × expected CPC — capped by available search demand and checked against the CPA you can afford.</div>

## Step by step

1. **Customers needed per month** — from your revenue target.
2. **Lead-to-customer rate** — from your CRM.
3. **Landing-page conversion rate** — from analytics (or a conservative estimate).
4. **Expected CPC** — from Google Keyword Planner and your account history.
5. **Budget** = customers ÷ close rate ÷ conversion rate × CPC.
6. **Check affordability** — is the implied CPA below your break-even?
7. **Check demand** — does Keyword Planner show enough search volume to absorb it?

### Worked example (illustrative numbers)

- Need 10 customers; 10% of leads close → **100 leads**
- Landing page converts 5% of clicks → **2,000 clicks**
- Expected CPC ₹40 → **₹80,000 media per month**
- Implied CPA: ₹8,000. If a customer is worth ₹25,000 gross profit, that works.

## The learning-phase constraint

Automated bid strategies need conversion data to optimise. If your budget produces only a handful of conversions a month, consider optimising to a higher-volume qualified action (e.g. qualified lead) or consolidating campaigns so each gets enough data.

## Where budgets go wrong

- Spreading a small budget across too many campaigns
- Running PMax without brand exclusions and counting brand demand as growth
- Ignoring lost impression share due to rank — more budget won't fix poor relevance
